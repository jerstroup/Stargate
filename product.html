// PRODUCT · AGENT SYSTEM

A product team of agents that ships what users actually need

Seven agents that work like a small product org. Two listen to users and the market. One decides what to build. One writes specs that engineering can act on without guessing. One ships and runs the experiments. One measures what changed. An orchestrator keeps every bet tied to a measurable outcome. Copy the setup prompt into Claude. It will interview you about your product and then write the whole system as a folder of agent files.

The goal the system works toward

Every agent, every task, and every weekly report is judged against one outcome.

// north star

Ship the right things: every release targets a real, evidenced user problem and measurably improves activation, retention, or expansion.

The system counts a feature as a win when users change their behavior, not when it ships. You pick the north-star metric during setup, for example weekly active teams doing the core action, or successful transactions. Every spec states the result it's expected to move, and every launch is checked against that result.

Primary
North-star metric (defined in setup)
Leading

No spec without evidence · no launch without a metric

How the agents work together

The orchestrator sets the week's focus. Work moves from discovering problems to deciding which to solve, then defining, building and shipping. Product Analytics measures usage at every step and sends a scorecard back, so the next roadmap decision is based on what changed for users. Click any agent to jump to its role.

The agents and what each one does

Each agent becomes its own markdown file. The defaults below are best practice. The setup interview adjusts them to your product, stage, team and tools.

// control

Product Orchestrator

Acts as head of product. Keeps the team focused on one outcome at a time, turns the scorecard into the week's priorities, records every decision and its reasons, and brings you only the calls that need you.

Does
  • Runs the weekly product cycle and keeps a decision log with the reasoning, the evidence and the decision that was rejected.
  • Adjusts to your stage. Before product-market fit it favors discovery and short bets. After it, it shifts to funnels, OKRs and experiments.
  • Turns one internal roadmap into three versions: internal (with risks), customer (outcome-led) and investor (focused on metrics).
Asks you
  • Company goal this quarter, team size and engineering capacity, how decisions get made today, and what you've already promised customers.
→ agents/product-orchestrator.md
// 01 · discover

Customer Discovery

Keeps a steady flow of real user evidence coming in, so the roadmap rests on what users do and say rather than on the loudest request.

Does
  • Writes interview guides that ask about past behavior, not hypotheticals, plus recruiting lists, with a target of 3–5 conversations a week.
  • Turns call transcripts, support tickets, NPS and reviews into tagged themes, with how often each comes up and direct quotes, not summaries.
  • Maintains the jobs-to-be-done and an opportunity tree that links each theme to its evidence.
Asks you
  • Who your users are, where feedback lives (calls, support tool, surveys), and whether it can read your call recordings and tickets.
→ agents/customer-discovery.md
// 01 · discover

Market & Competitive Intel

Watches the market so you can spot gaps instead of copying competitors' features.

Does
  • Tracks competitor changelogs, pricing pages, docs and review sites (G2, Capterra, app stores) every week.
  • Writes a 3-line summary each month: where they're ahead, where we're ahead, and where nobody is. The last line goes to the roadmap review.
  • Flags category shifts, new entrants and pricing moves that affect your bets.
Asks you
  • Your competitors and alternatives (including spreadsheets and doing nothing), your category, and anything you'd never build.
→ agents/market-intel.md
// 02 · decide

Prioritization & Roadmap

Decides what not to build, which is the hardest call in product, and makes the reasons visible.

Does
  • Scores opportunities with RICE. Confidence is tied to the strength of the evidence, not to gut feel.
  • Adds usage data from Product Analytics and, for B2B, revenue at stake from the CRM.
  • Keeps a Now / Next / Later roadmap linked to goals, plus an explicit not-doing list.
Asks you
  • Quarterly goals or OKRs, engineering capacity, current roadmap, and customer commitments.
→ agents/prioritization-roadmap.md
// 03 · define & ship

Spec Writer

Turns a chosen opportunity into a spec that engineering and design (people or agents) can build from without guessing.

Does
  • Enforces one template: problem with quotes, who it's for, outcome, acceptance criteria, non-goals, open questions, and one success metric with a baseline (see the example spec.md).
  • Writes user stories with testable Given / When / Then criteria, edge cases, analytics events and a rollout plan.
  • Refuses to publish a spec that's missing a section. It hands off to the Engineering planner and the Design system.
Asks you
  • Your issue tracker (Linear, Jira, GitHub Issues), your design tool, and any spec format your team already uses.
→ agents/spec-writer.md
// 03 · define & ship

Launch & Experiments

Ships each feature as an experiment, so every release teaches you something even when it misses.

Does
  • Designs the experiment: hypothesis, main metric, a metric that must not get worse, sample size and duration. When traffic is too low for an A/B test, it uses a before/after comparison plus user interviews.
  • Plans a staged rollout behind a feature flag (beta group, then 25%, then 100%) and runs the launch checklist.
  • Drafts release notes and the weekly changelog in your brand voice. It runs reviews at 2 and 6 weeks to decide whether to keep, change or remove the feature.
Asks you
  • Feature-flag tool, release process, beta users, and where announcements go (in-app, email, changelog).
→ agents/launch-experiments.md
// measure & learn

Product Analytics

Defines what "working" means and proves whether each release moved it.

Does
  • Pins down the north-star metric, the activation moment (when users first get value), and the retention definition.
  • If you have no stack yet, recommends one: PostHog as the default (analytics, flags, replays and surveys), with Amplitude or Mixpanel as alternatives. If you already have one, asks you to connect it read-only, or works from exports.
  • Owns the event naming scheme (object_action) and sends a weekly scorecard: funnel, cohorts and results from launches.
Asks you
  • Your analytics tool and data warehouse, whether you can connect them read-only, and any privacy or compliance limits (GDPR, HIPAA, SOC 2).
→ agents/product-analytics.md

// always needs your approval

  • Roadmap changes and anything promised to customers
  • A spec moving to "ready for engineering"
  • Launching experiments or features to real users
  • Contacting customers (interview outreach, surveys)
  • Pricing or packaging changes, and access to personal data

// red flags to watch

  • Features ship but usage stays flat: the problem wasn't real or wasn't urgent
  • Roadmap driven by your loudest customer: there are too few interviews behind it
  • Specs with no baseline metric: you can't tell whether a release worked
  • Interviewing only fans: recruit churned and lost users too
