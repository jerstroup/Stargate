// SUPPORT · AGENT SYSTEM

A support team of agents that makes two people feel like twenty

Seven agents that work like a small customer-experience team. One keeps the knowledge base true and current. One sorts every incoming ticket. One drafts answers that cite their sources. One turns bugs into issues engineering can act on. One keeps customers healthy so they stay. One measures quality and tells the rest of the company what customers are saying. An orchestrator keeps the queue moving. Your people spend their time on the 10% of conversations that need a human. Copy the setup prompt into Claude. It will interview you about your support operation and then write the whole system as a folder of agent files.

The goal the system works toward

Every agent, every task, and every weekly report is judged against one outcome.

// north star

Every customer gets a fast, accurate, honest resolution, and what they tell us makes the product better, so they stay.

Closing tickets isn't the win; solving problems so they don't come back is. "Deflection" counts only when the customer doesn't return with the same issue. New customers arrive from the Sales agent system's handoff. Bugs go to Engineering, and themes from customer conversations go to Product's Customer Discovery agent.

Primary
Resolved on first contact, not reopened within 7 days
Speed
First response < 1 hr · P1 bug → issue < 1 hr
Retention
CSAT · gross revenue retention
Guardrail
AI answers cite a doc · a human is one click away

How the agents work together

The orchestrator sets the queue's priorities for the day and the week. Conversations arrive from customers and from the Sales handoff, get triaged against the knowledge base, and are resolved. When a fix is needed they're escalated to engineering, and the account is kept healthy so the customer stays. Quality & Insights samples conversations and sends a scorecard back, so the next week's plan is based on what actually helped customers. Click any agent to jump to its role.

The agents and what each one does

Each agent becomes its own markdown file. The defaults below are best practice. The setup interview adjusts them to your product, ticket volume, channels, team and tools.

// control

Support Orchestrator

Acts as head of support. Keeps the queue healthy, matches coverage to demand, decides what the agents handle and what goes to people, and brings you only the decisions that need you.

Does
  • Runs a daily queue check (backlog, tickets at risk of missing their response-time target, escalations) and a weekly review using the quality scorecard.
  • Sets what AI may handle: which topics get automatic answers, which get drafts for a person to review, and which always go straight to a person.
  • Collects refunds, credits, exceptions and promises to customers into one approval list. Shares themes with Product and bugs with Engineering.
Asks you
  • Ticket volume and channels, team size and hours, response-time targets (SLAs), and what customers may and may not be promised.
→ agents/support-orchestrator.md
// 01 · intake

Knowledge Base

Keeps the knowledge base true and current, because a support agent is only as good as the docs it cites.

Does
  • Keeps one article per topic, each with frontmatter: owner, last-verified date, scope and tags (see the example KB article).
  • Flags articles that haven't been verified in 90 days. Those drop out of the set the AI is allowed to cite until someone checks them.
  • Spots gaps: questions that keep coming up with no article get a drafted article for review.
Asks you
  • Where your docs live (help center, Notion, git), your refund and billing policies, known issues, and who owns each area.
→ agents/knowledge-base.md
// 01 · intake

Triage & Routing

Sorts every incoming conversation before a person sees it, so the urgent ones never wait.

Does
  • Labels each ticket (how-to, bug, billing, feature request, churn risk, security) and sets its priority.
  • Attaches the customer's context: plan, account value, health and their last 3 tickets.
  • Sends security issues and churn risks to a person within 5 minutes, at any hour. Everything else goes to the right queue or owner.
Asks you
  • Your helpdesk (Intercom, Zendesk, Help Scout, Front, shared inbox), your priority levels and response targets, and your on-call rotation.
→ agents/triage-routing.md
// 02 · resolve

Resolution & Replies

Writes accurate, warm answers quickly, and is honest about when to hand over to a person.

Does
  • Drafts replies from the knowledge base and the customer's history, and cites the article it used. If there's no article to cite, it doesn't send.
  • AI-first replies only on topics you've approved. They say they're from AI, offer "talk to a person" in every reply, and hand over to a person when the AI isn't confident.
  • Summarizes support calls and drafts follow-ups. It can suggest a refund or a delivery date but never commits to one.
Asks you
  • Your support voice, the topics AI may answer on its own, and whether drafts need review or may be sent automatically.
→ agents/resolution-replies.md
// 03 · fix & retain

Escalation & Bug Loop

Turns customer pain into fixes. This is usually the agent that pays off most.

Does
  • Turns bug tickets into issues Engineering can act on: reproduction steps, environment, affected accounts, and linked error logs (e.g. from Sentry).
  • Checks for an existing issue first. If there is one, it adds the customer to a notify-when-fixed list, and it drafts the "it's fixed" message when the fix ships.
  • During an outage, drafts status-page updates and messages to affected customers for a person to approve.
Asks you
  • Your issue tracker (Linear, Jira, GitHub), your error tracking, your status page, and your bug severity levels.
→ agents/escalation-bugs.md
// 03 · fix & retain

Customer Success & Retention

Makes sure customers reach value and stay. It picks up where the Sales handoff ends.

Does
  • Turns the Sales handoff document into an onboarding plan with goals, check-ins, and every promise made during the sale.
  • Keeps a health score (usage, support load, sentiment, payment status) and triggers a playbook when an account is at risk.
  • Prepares renewal and business-review packs from data, and spots expansion opportunities to pass back to Sales.
Asks you
  • Who owns accounts after the sale, your customer-success tool if any, renewal terms, and what signs of a healthy customer look like.
→ agents/customer-success.md
// measure & learn

Quality & Insights

Measures what customers actually experienced and turns support into a source of product insight.

Does
  • Has a person review 20 random AI-handled conversations each week. Counts a ticket as deflected only if the customer didn't come back with the same issue within 48 hours.
  • Tracks first-response time, resolution time, reopen rate, CSAT, how accurate escalations were, and how long bugs take to get fixed.
  • Sends a weekly voice-of-customer digest that highlights changes rather than only the top complaints, to Product's Customer Discovery agent.
Asks you
  • The metrics your helpdesk reports, whether you run CSAT or NPS, and access to read or export conversations.
→ agents/quality-insights.md

// always needs your approval

  • Refunds, credits, discounts and billing changes
  • Changes to a customer's account, data or access
  • Promises about dates, features or SLAs
  • Expanding which topics AI answers on its own
  • Outage messages, status-page posts and security replies

// red flags to watch

  • Deflection is up but repeat contacts are up too: the AI is closing tickets without solving them
  • Lots of uncited drafts: the knowledge base has gaps or out-of-date articles
  • People rewrite more than half of drafts: the voice file or KB is too thin
  • The same bug keeps getting reported: the notify-and-fix loop is broken
  • CSAT is fine but churn is rising: you're only hearing from happy customers
