// LEGAL & COMPLIANCE · AGENT SYSTEM

A legal desk of agents that keeps deals moving and you audit-ready

Seven agents that work like a fractional legal and compliance team. Two review what comes in: contracts, plus the data-protection terms attached to them. One keeps your security and compliance evidence current. Two watch the rules and keep your policies and IP clean. One tracks every obligation and deadline. An orchestrator sorts incoming requests and sends anything risky to a real attorney. The agents do the drafting. Your lawyer makes the judgment calls and you sign. Copy the setup prompt into Claude. It will interview you about your company and then write the whole system as a folder of agent files.

contract review privacy & DPAs SOC 2 & questionnaires regulatory radar policies & IP obligations & risk
Not legal advice. These agents prepare, compare and flag. They don't give legal opinions. A qualified attorney in the relevant jurisdiction must review anything binding, anything high-risk, and anything relied on outside the company. Agents never sign, never send redlines or legal positions to a counterparty, and never make filings or notifications.

The goal the system works toward

Every agent, every task, and every weekly report is judged against one outcome.

// north star

Close deals at speed and stay audit-ready year-round, with no missed obligations and every binding or high-risk decision reviewed by the right human.

The system doesn't count drafts produced as wins. What matters is how fast a contract is ready to sign, whether your controls have current evidence, and that nothing falls through the cracks. Non-standard terms arrive from the Sales deal desk. Vendor and employment paperwork comes from Operations. Obligations with money attached, such as renewals, caps and credits, are shared with Finance.

How the agents work together

The orchestrator takes in every request, sorts it by risk, and sends high-risk matters to your attorney. Work moves from reviewing what comes in, to keeping you compliant, to staying ahead of new rules. Contracts arrive from Sales. The Obligations & Risk agent tracks every commitment and deadline and sends a scorecard back, so next week's priorities follow your real exposure. Click any agent to jump to its role.

The agents and what each one does

Each agent becomes its own markdown file. The defaults below are best practice. The setup interview adjusts them to your jurisdictions, industry, contract volume, risk tolerance and tools.

// control

Legal Orchestrator

Acts as the front door of a fractional general counsel's office. Every request comes in here and gets a risk tier and an owner. Routine work stays with the agents. Anything binding, high-risk or outside the playbook goes to your attorney with a short brief.

Does
  • Runs a single intake (an email alias or Slack channel) and a matter tracker: request, type, risk tier, owner and due date.
  • Sorts every matter into one of three tiers: standard (you approve), negotiated within fallbacks (you approve, with a memo) and outside the playbook or high-risk (attorney review required).
  • Prepares escalation packets so your lawyer is billed for judgment, not for catching up on the background.
Asks you
  • Who handles legal today (founder, fractional GC, outside counsel), your risk tolerance, and who must review what.
→ agents/legal-orchestrator.md
// 01 · review

Contract Review

Gives every incoming contract a first pass in minutes, at the level of a junior associate's first draft, measured against your playbook.

Does
  • Compares each clause to your contract playbook (hard lines, fallbacks, acceptable concessions) and rates it acceptable, negotiate or escalate (see the example playbook.md).
  • Flags risky language such as uncapped liability, broad IP assignment, one-sided indemnities, MFN or exclusivity clauses, and training AI on your data. It also flags clauses that are missing.
  • Drafts suggested redlines plus a short "what I'd push back on" memo. A person sends anything back to the other side.
Asks you
  • The contracts you sign most, whether you have your own templates, the positions you already hold, and your e-signature and storage tools.
→ agents/contract-review.md
// 01 · review

Privacy & Data Protection

Keeps your privacy promises consistent with what you actually do. Enterprise deals often hinge on the data processing agreement (DPA).

Does
  • Maintains the data map: what personal data you hold, why, where it lives, who sees it and how long you keep it. It also keeps a list of sub-processors (vendors that handle that data) with their regions.
  • Fills in customer DPAs from the data map, and flags EU data transfers and sub-processor changes that require notifying customers.
  • Runs the process for data subject access requests (DSARs), where people ask to see or delete their data, and checks that the privacy policy matches practice.
Asks you
  • What personal data you handle (any sensitive categories), where your users are, and your current privacy policy and vendors.
→ agents/privacy-data.md
// 02 · comply

Compliance & Trust

Turns security reviews from a 2-week scramble into a 2-hour task and keeps you ready for an audit.

Does
  • Plans the path to SOC 2 or ISO 27001 when buyers start asking, and keeps a calendar of evidence to collect for each control.
  • Drafts security-questionnaire answers from an approved answer bank, with citations. Where your current setup falls short of a question, it routes it to a person with a proposed fix.
  • Maintains the incident-response and breach-notification plan. Whether an incident must be reported is decided by an attorney.
Asks you
  • Any certifications you hold or are targeting, your compliance tool (Vanta, Drata, Secureframe), and how often questionnaires come in.
→ agents/compliance-trust.md
// 03 · anticipate

Regulatory Radar

Watches the rules that apply to you, so new obligations don't catch you by surprise.

Does
  • Each month, scans for changes relevant to your jurisdictions and industry: privacy laws, AI regulation (e.g. the EU AI Act), and sector rules.
  • Writes a one-pager for each change: what changed, whether it applies to you, the deadline, a suggested action and how confident it is. These go to you and your counsel.
  • Sends needed policy updates to Policy & IP Hygiene.
Asks you
  • Where you operate and where your users are, your industry and any regulators, and whether you build with or sell AI.
→ agents/regulatory-radar.md
// 03 · anticipate

Policy & IP Hygiene

Prevents the slow-burn problems that surface during due diligence: unsigned IP assignments, inconsistent terms and license violations.

Does
  • Keeps the policy and template library under version control: terms of service, privacy policy, acceptable use policy, NDA, contractor and employee agreements.
  • Checks that every employee and contractor has signed an IP assignment, scans code for open-source licenses (copyleft licenses such as GPL and AGPL get reviewed), and tracks trademarks.
  • Tracks how users accept the terms (ToS version and consent logs) and corporate housekeeping: annual reports, franchise tax, board consents. It works with Operations.
Asks you
  • Your entity type and state, HR and code-repository tools, and the status of current templates and trademarks.
→ agents/policy-ip-hygiene.md
// measure & learn

Obligations & Risk

Makes sure nothing you've signed or committed to gets missed, and shows the orchestrator where your real exposure is.

Does
  • Keeps a contract register for every signed agreement: counterparty, value, term, auto-renewal, notice windows, key obligations and non-standard terms.
  • Runs the deadline calendar (renewal notices, filings, certification audits, policy reviews) and a risk register scored by likelihood × impact.
  • Produces a weekly scorecard on turnaround time, contracts on your own paper, evidence freshness and upcoming deadlines. It shares money-related obligations with Finance.
Asks you
  • Where signed contracts live, whether it can read your e-signature and storage tools (read-only), and which deadlines you already know about.
→ agents/obligations-risk.md

Your attorney still makes the call. Use the agents for drafting, comparing, tracking and flagging, which is the work that eats billable hours. Use your lawyer for judgment: whether to accept a risk, whether an incident must be reported, how to read a new law, and anything you'll sign or file. Using AI doesn't make communications privileged. // always needs a human (and often an attorney)

  • Signing anything, or sending redlines or legal positions to another party
  • Accepting terms outside the playbook or tagged high-risk
  • Filings with any government, court or agency
  • Breach or incident notifications to regulators or customers
  • Publishing or changing the terms of service, privacy policy or other public policies
  • Any legal conclusion relied on outside the company

// red flags to watch

  • Most contracts are on the customer's paper: you need your own templates
  • The same clause gets escalated again and again: the playbook needs a position on it
  • Stale evidence before an audit: the evidence calendar isn't being worked
  • An auto-renewal passed without review: the obligations register is incomplete
  • A contractor without a signed IP assignment: fix it now, before due diligence finds it
