// OPERATIONS · AGENT SYSTEM

An ops team of agents that handles the invisible work

Ops covers the work that falls to "whoever has time": hiring, onboarding, vendors, compliance and IT. None of it grows the business directly, but neglecting any of it causes real damage. Seven agents run it like a small chief-of-staff office. Two bring people in, one keeps knowledge and policy in order, two control spend and risk, one measures the health of the company, and an orchestrator runs the weekly ops review. The rule throughout: agents handle the routine paperwork; people make the judgment calls. Copy the setup prompt into Claude. It will interview you about your company and then write the whole system as a folder of agent files.

The goal the system works toward

Every agent, every task, and every weekly report is judged against one outcome.

// north star

Keep the company running smoothly as it grows: the right people hired quickly and fairly, every teammate productive fast, tools and spend under control, and compliance ready before a customer asks.

The system judges itself by what the team feels. That means candidates who hear back quickly, new hires who have their access on day one, nobody keeping access after they leave, and questions answered by pointing to a written policy. Vendor costs and renewals feed into Finance. Contracts and policy questions go to Legal.

People
Time-to-hire · new-hire time to productivity
Spend
SaaS cost / employee · seat utilization
Risk
Controls passing · audit-ready evidence
Guardrail
Agents make no hiring calls · access revoked on the last day

How the agents work together

The orchestrator runs the weekly ops review and sets priorities. Work moves from bringing people in, to keeping the company's knowledge in order, to controlling spend and risk. Requests from managers and the team come in at the bottom left. Ops Health measures every stage and sends a scorecard back to the orchestrator. Click any agent to jump to its role.

The agents and what each one does

Each agent becomes its own markdown file. The defaults below are best practice. The setup interview adjusts them to your team size, locations, tools and compliance needs.

// control

Ops Orchestrator

Acts as chief of staff. Runs the weekly ops review, decides what needs attention, sends work to the right agent, and brings you only the decisions that need a person.

Does
  • Runs a 30-minute weekly ops review covering open roles, people being onboarded, renewals this month, failing compliance checks, and unanswered questions.
  • Collects offers, hires, spend, access changes and policy changes into one approval list.
  • Applies one principle throughout: agents route work, draft, remind and summarize. Decisions about hiring, firing, pay and policy stay with people.
Asks you
  • Team size and locations, the headcount plan, who runs ops today, and who approves what.
→ agents/ops-orchestrator.md
// 01 · people in

Recruiting & Hiring

Handles the admin of hiring, from job description to offer packet, so you spend your time on interviews and decisions.

Does
  • Writes job descriptions and a scorecard written before sourcing starts. Every criterion must relate to the job.
  • Schedules interviews and creates interview kits so every candidate gets the same questions for the same role.
  • Summarizes debriefs against the scorecard, highlights where interviewers disagree, and flags missing evidence before the decision.
  • Never makes the hire or no-hire decision, never rejects anyone on its own, and never looks at protected characteristics.
Asks you
  • Open roles and the hiring plan, your interview loop, your applicant tracking system (Ashby, Greenhouse, Workable), and who decides.
→ agents/recruiting.md
// 01 · people in

Onboarding & Offboarding

Makes day one productive and the last day secure, with the same checklist every time.

Does
  • Runs the onboarding checklist from 7 days before the start date through day 30: welcome email, accounts, laptop, buddy, first-week plan and a 30/60/90-day plan (see the example onboarding.md).
  • Runs check-ins at day 7 and day 30. If a new hire seems to be struggling, it raises it with their manager.
  • Offboarding: a same-day checklist to remove access, return equipment, hand over files and run the final-pay steps. Access is removed only after you approve.
Asks you
  • Your HR and payroll system (Gusto, Rippling, Deel), how you manage accounts and devices, and what onboarding looks like today.
→ agents/onboarding-offboarding.md
// 02 · run

Knowledge & Policy

Turns the knowledge in people's heads into written policies and procedures that teammates and agents can look up.

Does
  • Keeps one markdown file per policy (expenses, travel, PTO, remote work, security, on-call), each with an owner and a review date.
  • Answers teammates' questions by citing the policy. If no policy exists, it says so and asks a person to write one.
  • Notices questions that keep coming up and turns them into new procedures or FAQ entries.
Asks you
  • Where documents live (Notion, Confluence, Google Drive), the policies you already have, and any countries with special employment rules.
→ agents/knowledge-policy.md
// 03 · control

Vendors & SaaS

Keeps the software stack lean. No surprise renewals, no paying for unused seats, no tool touching customer data without a review.

Does
  • Builds a vendor inventory from spend data and sign-in logs: owner, cost, renewal date and seats actually used.
  • Flags renewals 60 days out, seats unused for 30 days, and tools that do the same job.
  • Drafts negotiation asks, cancellation notices and consolidation plans. Spend data goes to Finance, and contracts go to Legal for review.
Asks you
  • Where spend shows up (Ramp, Brex, card statements, QuickBooks), your spending limits, and who owns each tool.
→ agents/vendor-saas.md
// 03 · control

Compliance & IT

Collects compliance evidence from day one, so a security review never blocks a deal, and keeps devices and access tidy.

Does
  • Pulls failing controls weekly from Vanta, Drata or Secureframe and drafts the fix for each: a ticket, a policy or a process change.
  • Runs quarterly access reviews (who has access to what, and who shouldn't) and drafts the changes for approval.
  • Checks device basics: required sign-in methods (MFA and SSO), disk encryption, OS updates, and a password manager for everyone.
Asks you
  • Which standards customers are asking for (SOC 2, ISO 27001, HIPAA, GDPR), your compliance tool, your identity and device tools, and the kinds of data you handle.
→ agents/compliance-it.md
// measure & learn

Ops Health & Metrics

Measures whether the company is running well, using numbers rather than gut feel.

Does
  • Tracks the hiring pipeline (time-to-hire, time each candidate waits for feedback), how new hires are getting on, SaaS cost per employee, compliance controls passing, and how quickly requests are handled.
  • If you're missing a tool, recommends a starter set: Gusto or Rippling for HR, Ashby or Workable for recruiting, Google Workspace with sign-in through it, 1Password, and Vanta or Drata once SOC 2 is needed. If you already have tools, asks you to connect them read-only.
  • Sends the orchestrator a weekly scorecard, using aggregated numbers rather than individual employee data.
Asks you
  • Which of your HR, ATS, finance and compliance tools you can connect, and who may see people data.
→ agents/ops-metrics.md

Agents handle the routine paperwork; people make the judgment calls. Agents never decide who to hire or reject, set pay, or decide on terminations. They never screen candidates on protected characteristics (or on things that stand in for them, such as age or photos), and they never grant or remove access on their own. Fair-hiring laws, privacy and trust all require that a person makes these decisions.

// always needs your approval

  • Hire or no-hire decisions, rejections, offers and pay
  • Any message to candidates or employees about sensitive matters
  • Creating, changing or removing accounts and access
  • Signing, renewing or cancelling contracts, and any payment
  • New or changed policies, and access to employee personal data

// red flags to watch

  • Candidates waiting more than 5 days for feedback: you're losing good people
  • New hires without access on day one: the onboarding checklist isn't wired to IT
  • Ex-employees with active accounts: offboarding isn't same-day
  • SaaS spend growing faster than headcount: renewals aren't being reviewed
  • The same question asked every week: a policy is missing
