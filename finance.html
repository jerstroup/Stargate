// FINANCE · AGENT SYSTEM

A finance team of agents, so you always know your numbers

Seven agents that work like a fractional finance team. Two keep the books and the cash coming in. One builds the model and runs the "what if" questions. Two keep spending in check and turn the numbers into investor and board updates. One watches cash and metrics every day. An orchestrator runs the close and brings you the decisions. Agents never move money. They read, reconcile, model and draft, and you approve and execute. Copy the setup prompt into Claude. It will interview you about your company and then write the whole system as a folder of agent files.

bookkeeping & close AR / AP financial model scenarios & runway spend control investor updates metrics

The goal the system works toward

Every agent, every task, and every weekly report is judged against one outcome.

// north star

Know your numbers cold and never be surprised by cash: books closed accurately and on time, runway always known, and every spending or fundraising decision backed by a model you trust.

Investors don't expect a perfect model. They expect a founder who knows the numbers. The system keeps one set of metric definitions, one live model and one cash view, and every update, board deck and decision draws from the same source. Revenue forecasts start from the Sales agent system's pipeline, and the numbers feed your fundraising work.

Primary
Runway (months), known weekly and above target
Discipline
Monthly close in ≤ 10 business days
Accuracy
Forecast vs. actual within ±10%
Guardrail
Agents never move money · burn multiple tracked

How the agents work together

The orchestrator runs the weekly review and the monthly close. Work moves from recording what happened to planning what's next, then to controlling spend and reporting results. Bookings come in from Sales. Metrics & Cash Monitor syncs actuals every day and sends a scorecard back, so decisions are always based on current numbers. Click any agent to jump to its role.

The agents and what each one does

Each agent becomes its own markdown file. The defaults below are best practice. The setup interview adjusts them to your stage, business model, team and tools.

// control

Finance Orchestrator

Acts as a fractional CFO. Runs a 15-minute weekly numbers review and the monthly close calendar, coordinates with your bookkeeper and CPA, and brings you at most three decisions at a time, each with a clear ask.

Does
  • Runs the rhythm: daily actuals sync, a weekly review, a monthly close and investor update, and a quarterly board pack. Agents draft all of it and you decide.
  • Matches the work to your stage. Before Series A it models 18 months of runway plus a plan for the next 6 months, not a 5-year fantasy.
  • Collects every payment, journal entry, filing and external report into one approval list.
Asks you
  • Stage and next fundraising milestone, cash on hand and burn, who keeps the books today, and how often investors and the board expect updates.
→ agents/finance-orchestrator.md
// 01 · record

Bookkeeping & Close

Keeps the books clean every day so the month-end close takes days, not weeks.

Does
  • Categorizes transactions in QuickBooks or Xero each night and flags any it isn't confident about. It reconciles bank, card and Stripe accounts.
  • Runs the month-end close checklist: accruals, deferred revenue, prepaid expenses, payroll, and a flux review (why each line changed).
  • Keeps the tax and compliance calendar, including franchise tax, annual reports, sales-tax nexus, 1099s and 83(b) deadlines. It reminds you 30 days ahead and prepares work for your CPA, but never files.
Asks you
  • Accounting software, your bookkeeper and CPA, entity type and state, and whether you use cash or accrual accounting.
→ agents/bookkeeping-close.md
// 01 · record

Billing & Collections

Gets cash in on time and keeps bills organized, without ever pressing "pay".

Does
  • Turns new deals from Sales into invoices, tracks how long invoices have been unpaid, and drafts polite reminders at 3, 7, 14 and 30 days for a person to send.
  • Reads vendor invoices from the inbox, matches them to contracts or purchase orders, and flags duplicates or price changes.
  • Prepares a weekly bill-payment batch for your approval. You make the payments.
Asks you
  • Billing tool (Stripe, QuickBooks invoicing, Chargebee), payment terms, bill-pay tool (Bill.com, Ramp), and who approves bills.
→ agents/billing-collections.md
// 02 · plan

Financial Model & Scenarios

Keeps one live, driver-based model. When you ask "what if we hire 3 engineers?", it answers in minutes.

Does
  • Builds the model in Google Sheets with tabs for inputs, drivers, P&L, cash flow, headcount and scenarios. Formulas use named ranges, not cell addresses.
  • Refreshes the forecast each month from actuals and from the Sales pipeline, using base, upside and downside cases.
  • Turns plain-English scenarios into driver changes and reports four numbers: runway, cash-out date, ARR at the next raise, and burn multiple. Every scenario is saved.
Asks you
  • Any existing model, your hiring plan, revenue drivers, and the timing and size of your next raise.
→ agents/model-scenarios.md
// 03 · control & report

Spend Control

Makes sure every dollar of burn is intentional, and catches mistakes and fraud early.

Does
  • Compares budget to actual spending by team each month and explains any line that's off by more than 10%.
  • Audits software subscriptions and vendors: duplicates, unused seats, and renewals coming up in the next 60 days.
  • Watches for anomalies such as double charges, refund spikes, a card used in a new country, or a payroll or bank-detail change you didn't approve, and alerts you immediately.
Asks you
  • Card and spend platform (Ramp, Brex, Mercury), your budget, and the spending amount above which you want to approve purchases.
→ agents/spend-control.md
// 03 · control & report

Investor & Board Reporting

Turns the monthly investor update into a 20-minute job, with the facts already correct, so you can focus on the story.

Does
  • Drafts the monthly update in five sections: Headline, KPIs, Highlights, Lowlights and Asks. Investors read the Asks first, so they're always real requests.
  • Builds the quarterly board pack and highlights the metrics investors focus on at your stage.
  • Supports fundraising with data-room financials, metric backups and answers to due-diligence questions.
Asks you
  • Your investor list, update and board schedule, and past updates so it can match the format.
→ agents/investor-reporting.md
// measure & learn

Metrics & Cash Monitor

Keeps one definition for every metric and one up-to-date view of cash, so no number means three things to three people.

Does
  • Maintains the metric dictionary: ARR, gross margin, gross and net burn, net revenue retention, CAC payback and burn multiple, each defined precisely (see the example metrics.md).
  • Syncs actuals every day from the bank, Stripe and payroll through read-only connections, and alerts you when something is more than 20% off forecast.
  • Sends a weekly scorecard: cash, burn, runway, revenue, and the forecast compared with actuals.
Asks you
  • Your bank, payment processor and payroll provider, and whether you can connect them read-only (or share exports).
→ agents/metrics-cash-monitor.md

Agents never move money, and this isn't tax or accounting advice. In this system agents read, reconcile, model and draft. You (or your bookkeeper) make every payment, transfer, payroll change and bank-setting change, and your CPA reviews and files every tax return.

// always needs your approval

  • Any payment, transfer or bill run (a person executes it)
  • Journal entries posted to the books, and closing a period
  • Tax filings and compliance submissions (your CPA files)
  • Investor updates, board packs and sharing financials externally
  • Budget changes and new vendor commitments over your threshold

// red flags to watch

  • The close takes more than 15 days: reconciliation is happening too late
  • The forecast misses by more than 20% two months running: the model's drivers are wrong
  • Runway under 12 months with no fundraising or cost plan
  • The same metric has different values in different decks: the dictionary isn't being used
  • Invoices unpaid for more than 60 days are piling up: collections have stalled
