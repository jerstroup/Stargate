// MARKETING · AGENT SYSTEM

A marketing team of agents with one job: qualified leads

Seven agents that work together like a small growth team. One studies your market, three bring in attention, one turns attention into leads, one measures everything, and an orchestrator keeps them all pointed at the same goal. Copy the setup prompt into Claude. It will interview you about your business and then write the whole system as a folder of agent files.

lead generation market insights SEO content & social paid ads nurture & CRM analytics

The goal the system works toward

Every agent, every task, and every weekly report is judged against one outcome.

// north star

A steady, measurable flow of qualified leads at a cost the business can afford, handed to sales fast enough to close.

You define "qualified" during setup. For B2B it might be a right-fit company that asked for a demo. For a local service it might be a quote request inside your service area. Agents don't count traffic, likes, or impressions as wins. Those numbers only matter when they turn into leads.

How the agents work together

The orchestrator plans the week and hands out work. Work moves left to right: understand, then attract, then convert. Analytics measures every stage and sends a scorecard back so next week's plan is based on what actually produced leads. Click any agent to jump to its role.

dispatch (orchestrator → agents) work flow (briefs, traffic, leads) data & feedback loop human approval

The agents and what each one does

Each agent becomes its own markdown file. The defaults below are best practice. The setup interview adjusts them to your industry, budget and tools.

// control

Growth Orchestrator

Acts as the head of marketing. Turns the goal into a weekly plan, hands out work, catches conflicts between agents, and brings you only the decisions that need you.

Does
  • Runs the weekly cycle: read the scorecard, choose priorities, brief agents.
  • Picks 1–2 primary channels and adds more only after one is working.
  • Collects everything waiting on you into one approval queue.
Asks you
  • What counts as a qualified lead, the monthly lead goal, and the hours per week you can spend reviewing.
→ agents/growth-orchestrator.md
// 01 · understand

Market & Customer Insights

Works out who buys, why they buy, and who you compete with. Every other agent builds on this research.

Does
  • Writes the ideal-customer profile, the pains customers describe, and the words they use.
  • Breaks down competitors: their positioning, offers, pricing, ads and content gaps.
  • Watches for demand signals in reviews, Reddit, forums and industry communities.
  • Suggests 3–5 messaging angles for the other agents to test.
Asks you
  • Industry, business and what you sell, your best customers, your top competitors, and your geography.
→ agents/market-insights.md
// 02 · attract

SEO

Reviews your website and makes it rank for searches that come from buyers, in Google and in AI assistants.

Does
  • Audits the site technically and on-page: indexing, titles, speed, mobile, schema and internal links.
  • Finds keywords that signal buying intent (service + city, "X vs Y", "X alternatives", pricing) before broad educational ones.
  • For local businesses: optimizes Google Business Profile, citations and review flow.
  • Turns findings into a prioritized fix list and a page plan.
Asks you
  • Your website URL, your CMS, target locations, and Search Console access (or an export).
→ agents/seo.md
// 02 · attract

Content & Social

Produces content in your voice that answers real buyer questions and ends with a lead offer.

Does
  • Keeps the brand-voice file current (see the example voice.md).
  • Plans a calendar of 1–2 strong pieces a week, each tied to a keyword or a customer question.
  • Turns each piece into LinkedIn, email and short-form social posts.
  • Drafts only. You supply or approve the point of view.
Asks you
  • Voice adjectives, writing samples you like, active channels, and any claims you can't make.
→ agents/content-social.md
// 02 · attract

Paid Ads

Recommends which ad platforms fit your business and budget, then builds campaigns ready to launch.

Does
  • Picks platforms by business model: Google Search for buyers already searching, Meta for B2C, LinkedIn for higher-value B2B, Local Services Ads for local businesses.
  • Builds the full campaign: structure, keywords, negative keywords, ad copy, assets, targeting and a landing-page brief.
  • If you connect Google Ads, it creates campaigns paused and you switch them on. Without a connection it delivers an import sheet.
  • Spends nothing until conversion tracking works.
Asks you
  • Monthly budget, deal value, target cost per lead, and existing ad accounts.
→ agents/paid-ads.md
// 03 · convert

Lead Capture & Nurture

Turns visitors into leads and keeps leads warm until they're ready for sales.

Does
  • Designs offers: one main call to action (demo, quote or consult) and one low-commitment lead magnet.
  • Specs short forms, CRM routing and an instant alert so leads get a response within 5 minutes.
  • Writes a 4–6 email nurture sequence and simple lead scoring based on fit and intent.
  • Defines the hand-off to sales, i.e. when a lead counts as qualified.
Asks you
  • Your CRM and email tool, how leads are followed up today, and your sales process.
→ agents/lead-nurture.md
// measure & learn

Analytics & Measurement

Makes sure every lead can be traced to its source, so the system shifts budget toward what works.

Does
  • If you have no stack yet, recommends a free one: GA4, Tag Manager, Search Console, Microsoft Clarity and Looker Studio.
  • If you already have accounts, asks you to connect them through Claude connectors, or works from CSV exports.
  • Writes the tracking plan (key events plus UTM naming) and checks it fires.
  • Sends a weekly scorecard to the orchestrator: leads by source, cost per lead, and what to stop, keep or scale.
Asks you
  • Which analytics, ads and CRM tools you have, and whether you can connect them read-only.
→ agents/analytics.md

// always needs your approval

  • Spending money (launching or raising ad budgets)
  • Publishing anything public
  • Sending email or messages to real people
  • Changing live accounts, the website, or tracking
  • Claims about results, pricing, or regulated topics

// red flags to watch

  • Traffic is up but leads aren't: wrong keywords or a weak offer
  • Leads but no customers: your "qualified" definition is too loose
  • Rising cost per lead: creative fatigue or broad targeting
  • Editors rewrite more than half of drafts: the voice file is too thin
