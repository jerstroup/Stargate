// FOUNDERS · AGENT SYSTEM

A CEO office of agents, so you can spend your time on decisions only you can make

Seven agents that work like the chief of staff, analysts and associates you can't afford yet. One watches the market. One sharpens your story. One runs your fundraise and investor relations. One reviews term sheets and contracts. One helps you hire the key people. One keeps the numbers ready for your board. A chief of staff ties them to your top priorities each week. You still decide, send and sign everything. Copy the setup prompt into Claude. It will interview you about your company and then write the whole system as a folder of agent files.

chief of staff market intel pitch & narrative fundraising term sheets & contracts key hires board & investor updates

The goal the system works toward

Every agent, every task, and every weekly report is judged against one outcome.

// north star

Hit the company's next milestone with the capital, people and focus to get there, while you spend your hours on the few decisions only you can make.

You name the milestone during setup, for example "close a $3M seed by June" or "reach $1M ARR with 18 months of runway". The system doesn't count decks produced or investors emailed as wins. Those only matter when they move the milestone. It works from the same numbers as the Finance and Data functions, so what investors see always matches the books.

Primary
Progress to the next milestone (on track / at risk)
Capital
Runway ≥ 18 months after a raise
Leverage
% of your hours spent on the top 3 priorities
Guardrail
Nothing sent or signed without you · numbers always sourced

How the agents work together

The chief of staff sets the week's priorities. Work moves from understanding the market and sharpening the story, to raising capital, to closing deals and hiring with it. Metrics & Board Reporting tracks progress toward the milestone and sends a scorecard back, so next week's priorities are based on what actually moved. Click any agent to jump to its role.

The agents and what each one does

Each agent becomes its own markdown file. The defaults below are best practice. The setup interview adjusts them to your stage, milestone, team and tools.

// control

Chief of Staff

The glue agent. Keeps your week pointed at the top 3 priorities, prepares you for every meeting, follows up after each one, and brings you only the decisions that need you.

Does
  • Sends a pre-brief 30 minutes before each meeting: who they are, your last interaction, three likely topics and two asks. After the meeting it drafts the follow-up and logs commitments as tasks.
  • Runs the Friday review: what slipped, what to cut, and what should go on next week's calendar.
  • Collects every draft, signature request and decision into one approval list, and tags each item with the agent that drafted it.
Asks you
  • Your next milestone and its date, your top 3 priorities this quarter, and how many hours a week you spend in meetings versus deep work.
→ agents/chief-of-staff.md
// 01 · sense & narrate

Market & Competitor Scout

Makes sure you're never the last to know what changed in your market.

Does
  • Every Monday, sends a 5-bullet brief: the most important competitor move, changes in how customers talk, new funding in your space, regulatory or platform changes, and one contrarian signal worth debating.
  • Keeps market.md current: market size with sources, the 5 closest competitors, and a matrix of how you differ.
  • Shares findings with the Marketing and Product systems.
Asks you
  • Your category, the competitors you watch, and the sources you trust (newsletters, communities, analyst reports).
→ agents/market-scout.md
// 01 · sense & narrate

Narrative & Pitch

Keeps one sharp company story and adapts it for every audience: investors, candidates, customers and the board.

Does
  • Maintains the one-pager (problem, customer, wedge, why now, team and ask; see the example one-pager.md) and keeps the one-liner under 140 characters.
  • Builds and tailors the deck to each fund's thesis and stage, and keeps the problem slide to one sentence.
  • Red-teams the deck before every partner meeting: the 15–20 hardest questions, a 1–5 score for each current answer, and one fix for each.
Asks you
  • Your current deck and one-pager (if any), your strongest proof points, and the story you tell when it goes well.
→ agents/narrative-pitch.md
// 02 · raise

Fundraising & Investor Relations

Runs your raise like a tight sales process, and keeps your current investors informed and helpful between raises.

Does
  • Builds a ranked list of 80–120 investors scored on stage, sector, check size, ability to lead, portfolio conflicts and recent activity, with a warm-intro path for each.
  • Runs the investor CRM from target to close, schedules first meetings in a 2–3 week batch, and drafts follow-ups within 24 hours.
  • Drafts the monthly investor update (highlights, metrics, lowlights, asks) and the data-room checklist.
Asks you
  • Raise amount, instrument (SAFE or priced round) and timing, your current investors, and where your warm network lives (LinkedIn, email, angels).
→ agents/fundraising-ir.md
// 03 · close & build

Deal Desk

Reads the fine print so you negotiate from a position of knowledge, and so your lawyer spends their hours on the hard parts.

Does
  • Compares term sheets to standard templates (YC post-money SAFE, NVCA) and flags non-standard terms: option-pool shuffles, participating preferred stock, liquidation preferences above 1×, full-ratchet anti-dilution, and shifts in board control.
  • Benchmarks leases and vendor MSAs. It flags personal guarantees, auto-renewal traps, IP assignment, data-handling terms and liability caps.
  • Never sends or signs. It produces a marked-up copy and a 5-point "what I'd push back on" memo for you and your lawyer.
Asks you
  • Upcoming negotiations (term sheet, lease, key vendors, partnerships), your lawyer, and the terms you won't accept.
→ agents/deal-desk.md
// 03 · close & build

Key Hires & Recruiting

Helps you land the few hires that define the company, and lets you spend your recruiting hours on closing candidates.

Does
  • Writes a scorecard for each role (outcomes for the first 6–12 months and the competencies needed) plus structured interview loops with consistent questions.
  • Builds sourcing lists and drafts personal outreach that you send. It runs reference-check guides and drafts offers within your compensation bands.
  • Follows fair-hiring rules and hands new hires to Operations for onboarding.
Asks you
  • The roles you need in the next 6–12 months, your compensation and equity bands, and your applicant tracking system (Ashby, Greenhouse, Gem) if you have one.
→ agents/key-hires.md
// measure & learn

Metrics & Board Reporting

Keeps one trusted set of numbers, so every deck, investor update and board meeting tells the same true story.

Does
  • Maintains metrics.md: revenue or ARR, growth, burn, runway, customer acquisition cost, retention and top customers. Each number carries its source and date.
  • If you're not connected yet, asks you to connect your accounting, billing and analytics tools read-only, or works from exports. It pulls from the Finance and Data systems when those exist.
  • Drafts the board pack (CEO letter, metrics, wins and lows, decisions needed) a week before each meeting, and sends a weekly milestone scorecard to the chief of staff.
Asks you
  • Your accounting and billing tools (QuickBooks, Xero, Stripe), your analytics tool, your board members and meeting cadence, and the metrics investors ask you about.
→ agents/metrics-board.md

// always needs your approval

  • Any message to investors, candidates, the board or partners
  • Signing, accepting or countering any term sheet, lease or contract
  • Sharing confidential information (cap table, financials, data room access)
  • Offers, compensation and equity grants
  • Any public statement about the raise or company metrics

// red flags to watch

  • Calendar full but priorities slipping: the chief of staff isn't cutting enough
  • Lots of first investor meetings but few second ones: the story or the metrics aren't landing
  • Deck numbers differ from the books: metrics.md isn't the single source of truth
  • Key hire still open after 90 days: the scorecard is unclear or you aren't spending enough founder time on it
  • Runway under 9 months with no raise underway: start fundraising now
